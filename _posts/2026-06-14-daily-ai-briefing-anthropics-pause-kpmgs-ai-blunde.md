### KPMG's AI Report Pulled Due to Hallucinations

In a classic case of "AI about AI" going wrong, consulting giant KPMG had to pull a report on AI usage because it contained apparent hallucinations. Yes, you read that right – an AI-generated report *about* AI usage was found to be unreliable.

This is a really important, practical lesson for anyone using AI in their work, especially for research or data gathering. While AI can be a powerful assistant, it's still prone to making things up. For businesses, this is a huge warning sign: always fact-check AI-generated content, especially when it's for public consumption or critical decision-making. The reputational damage from publishing inaccurate information, even if AI-generated, falls squarely on the human organization. Don't trust, verify!

### OpenAI Under Investigation by State Attorneys General

And finally, the biggest name in AI, OpenAI, is now facing an investigation from multiple state attorneys general. While the specific states involved aren't clear yet, the scope of the inquiry is broad, covering everything from OpenAI's ad policies to how it handles sensitive health data.

This is a significant development for anyone using OpenAI's tools, whether you're a developer building on their APIs or a company integrating their models into your workflow. It signals increased regulatory scrutiny over data privacy, transparency, and ethical use of AI. For businesses, this means it's more crucial than ever to understand the terms of service, data handling policies, and potential legal implications of using third-party AI models. Compliance and data governance are going to be key moving forward.
