### The Growing Debate Around AI Safety and Control

It's clear the AI world is taking safety, ethics, and control more seriously than ever. OpenAI, one of the leading AI research labs, just added Paul Christiano, an influential AI researcher focused on alignment (making sure AI systems do what we want them to do), to its board. This signals a deeper commitment to tackling the complex challenges of advanced AI systems. [OpenAI adds a prominent AI doomer to its board of directors](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/09/openai-adds-a-prominent-ai-doomer-to-its-board-of-directors/)

This move comes amidst growing concerns from within the industry itself. Connor Leahy of ControlAI recently described superintelligence not as a weapon, but as an "adversary," highlighting the potential dangers of deploying AI systems that are more capable than humans and difficult to control. [ControlAI’s Connor Leahy on why superintelligence is ‘not a weapon, it’s an adversary’](https://techcrunch.com/podcast/controlais-connor-leahy-on-why-superintelligence-is-not-a-weapon-its-an-adversary/)

Adding to this urgency, a researcher from Anthropic, Jacob Coxon, resigned over fears of AI extinction, calling for "pacing agreements" between labs to slow down development. These aren't just theoretical discussions; they're impacting real people working at the forefront of AI. For companies, this means that responsible AI development and robust safety protocols are no longer just good PR, but critical for long-term viability and public trust. For individuals, it's a reminder that the technology we're building has profound implications, and the industry is actively grappling with how to get it right. [‘Gambling with our lives’: Anthropic researcher quits, warns against self-improving AI](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/09/gambling-with-our-lives-anthropic-researcher-quits-warns-against-self-improving-ai/)

### Apple's AI Play: Privacy, Authenticity, and Always-Listening Tech

Apple is making a big splash with AI, integrating it across its hardware and software, but not without raising some eyebrows. The company is leaning into on-device AI models, with CEO John Ternus stating that the iPhone is still the best AI device, emphasizing the privacy benefits of processing data locally rather than in the cloud. [Apple CEO John Ternus says the best AI device is still the iPhone](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/09/apple-ceo-john-ternus-says-the-best-ai-device-is-still-the-iphone/)

On the privacy front, new Apple Watch AI features, which can transcribe recent speech and summarize ambient conversations, are sparking debate. While Apple assures users raw audio isn't saved, these capabilities normalize the idea that our devices could always be listening, raising significant questions about consent and how our behavior might change knowing we could be recorded. For device makers, balancing innovative features with user privacy is a tightrope walk. For users, it's crucial to understand what your devices are capable of and manage your privacy settings. [Apple Watch’s new AI features are normalizing the idea that technology is always listening](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/09/apple-watchs-new-ai-features-are-normalizing-the-idea-that-technology-is-always-listening/)

Beyond privacy, Apple is also tackling the growing issue of AI-generated content. They've introduced "Apple Reference Image," a new way to help users determine if photos have been edited, including alterations made by AI. This is a crucial step for maintaining trust in digital media and helping users prove their iPhone photos aren't "AI slop." For content creators and consumers alike, tools like this will be essential in a world increasingly filled with synthetic media. [Apple has a new new way to prove your iPhone photos aren’t AI slop](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/09/apple-has-a-new-way-prove-your-iphone-photos-arent-ai-slop/)

### AI Assistants Get Smarter and More Integrated

AI assistants are becoming incredibly practical, moving beyond simple commands to handle more complex tasks on our behalf. The viral AI assistant Instinct, for example, now has its own email address. This means it can create and manage accounts, contact businesses, and handle support requests, essentially acting as your digital proxy for a range of online interactions. Imagine the time saved! [Viral AI assistant Instinct now has its own email address](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/09/viral-ai-assistant-instinct-now-has-its-own-email-address/)

Similarly, Shipt, the delivery app, has rolled out its own AI shopping assistant. You can now give it natural language requests like "Create a cart for my Saturday tailgate for 25 people and include some brunch items," or "Build a cart for easy school lunches and after-school snacks." This isn't just about finding items; it's about understanding context and fulfilling complex, multi-item requests. For businesses, this means enhancing customer experience and efficiency. For us, it means less mental load for everyday chores. [Shipt becomes the latest delivery app with an AI shopping assistant](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/09/shipt-becomes-the-latest-delivery-app-with-an-AI-shopping-assistant/)
