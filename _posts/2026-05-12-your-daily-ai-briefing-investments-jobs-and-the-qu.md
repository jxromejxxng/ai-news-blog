Hey everyone, let's dive into today's AI trends! It's clear that AI isn't just a buzzword anymore; it's actively reshaping how money flows, how companies staff up, and even where we're looking to put our computing power. Here's a quick rundown of what caught my eye today.

## AI is Fueling New Investment Avenues

First up, if you're wondering where the big money is heading, look no further than Robinhood. The popular trading platform is confidentially filing for its second venture fund, and guess what? It's specifically targeting growth and early-stage startups. Given the current tech landscape, it's a safe bet a significant chunk of that capital will be flowing directly into innovative AI companies. This signals a continued strong belief in AI's future growth and a readiness to back the next wave of disruptive ideas. For founders, it means more potential avenues for funding; for everyone else, it means more AI-powered tools and services are likely on the horizon.
[TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/11/riding-an-ai-rally-robinhood-preps-second-retail-venture-ipo/)

## The AI Skill Shift: GM's Workforce Reshuffle

This next one is a big deal for anyone thinking about their career in tech. General Motors (GM) just laid off hundreds of IT workers, but here's the kicker: they're immediately looking to hire people with *stronger AI skills*. We're talking about roles in AI-native development, data engineering, cloud-based engineering, and even prompt engineering. This isn't just GM; it's a clear signal from a major industry player that the demand for traditional IT skills is evolving rapidly. If you're in tech, or looking to get in, this underscores the critical importance of upskilling in AI, data analytics, and cloud technologies. Companies are actively retooling their workforces to be AI-first.
[TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/11/gm-just-laid-off-hundreds-of-it-workers-to-hire-those-with-stronger-ai-skills/)

## Digg Tries Again, This Time with AI

Remember Digg? The social news aggregator from back in the day is making another comeback, and you guessed it, AI is at the core of its new strategy. The goal is to use AI to "track the most influential voices in a space" and surface the news that's actually worth your attention. In an age of information overload and endless feeds, an AI-powered aggregator that can genuinely cut through the noise and deliver truly relevant news would be a game-changer. If they can pull it off, it could redefine how many of us consume news, making our daily information diet far more efficient and insightful.
[TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/11/digg-tries-again-this-time-as-an-ai-news-aggregator/)

## AI's Insatiable Demand for Compute: Looking to Space

Finally, this story really highlights the sheer scale of AI's current demands. The push for more and more AI compute power is so intense that data center entrepreneurs are literally looking to the stars. The problem? There aren't enough rockets to put these crucial data centers into orbit, and existing options are too expensive. A company called Cowboy Space just raised a whopping $275 million to build more rockets for this very purpose. This isn't science fiction; it's a direct response to the "insatiable demand" for AI processing power. It tells us that the infrastructure needed to support advanced AI is becoming a bottleneck, and solving it requires thinking far beyond terrestrial solutions.
[TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/11/there-arent-enough-rockets-for-space-data-centers-cowboy-space-raised-275-million-to-build-them/)
