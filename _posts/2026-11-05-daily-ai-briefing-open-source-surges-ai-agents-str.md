## AI Agents: Still Finding Their Feet (and Their Use Cases)

We've heard a lot about AI agents doing everything for us, but the reality is a bit more nuanced.

First, a reality check: Uber announced that during its busiest week last quarter, only *four* people booked a ride using an AI agent. This shows that while the tech is cool, people often prefer simplicity and direct control for everyday tasks. Just because you *can* automate something with AI doesn't mean users *want* it that way. [Source: TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/04/almost-no-one-uses-ai-agents-to-book-their-uber-company-says/)

On the flip side, Meta is doubling down on AI agents for developers, acquiring a startup called Conductor. This company allows software engineers to deploy teams of AI agents to work on different aspects of a coding project simultaneously. This highlights a key distinction: while consumer-facing AI agents might struggle for widespread adoption, specialized AI agents that automate complex, repetitive tasks for professionals are proving to be incredibly valuable. [Source: TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/04/meta-acquires-ai-coding-startup-conductor/)

It's also worth noting that Apple is reportedly cutting budgets for its internal AI usage for engineering teams. This could be a sign of strategic refinement, or perhaps a realization that not every internal process needs a full-blown AI overhaul. It's a contrast to Meta's aggressive acquisition, showing that even tech giants are still figuring out the most effective ways to deploy AI internally. [Source: TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/04/apple-is-reportedly-cutting-budgets-for-its-internal-ai-usage/)

## The Open-Source AI Revolution is Gaining Steam

If you're a developer or run a business, this is a big one. Open-source AI models are booming, and companies like Hugging Face are at the center of it. The promise? Lower costs, more control, and greater flexibility compared to proprietary models. Enterprises, governments, and startups are increasingly relying on these freely available, powerful models.

This trend is democratizing AI, making advanced capabilities accessible to more players without massive upfront investments. However, it also raises questions about how companies capture value when their core technology is free, and how American players will compete with the rapid advancements in Chinese open models. [Source: TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/video/open-source-ai-is-booming-hugging-face-explains-who-profits/) and [Source: TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/podcast/building-on-open-source-ai-hugging-faces-policy-chief-on-the-costs-risks-and-trade-offs/)

For businesses, this means more options for integrating AI into products and workflows, potentially at a much lower cost. For developers, it means a richer ecosystem of tools and models to build with.

## New Gadgets & The Battle for Control in Education

The hardware space is heating up. Nothing, the smartphone startup, announced that its mysterious "AI-first device" is dropping next month. While details are scarce, the CEO promises it's designed to leverage AI for productivity gains. This signals a continued push to embed AI directly into our daily gadgets, moving beyond just apps and into the core device experience itself. [Source: TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/04/nothing-ceo-says-company%E2%80%99s-mysterious-ai-first-device-is-dropping-next-month/)

Meanwhile, a significant battle is brewing in the education sector. Google is facing backlash for a plan that would turn off Search for schools that restrict AI usage. This has drawn criticism from politicians, accusing Google of acting like an unregulated monopoly. [Source: TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/04/google-faces-backlash-for-plan-that-would-turn-off-search-for-schools-that-restrict-ai/)

Capitalizing on this frustration, DuckDuckGo is pitching itself to schools, highlighting its privacy-focused search engine and options to disable AI features. This situation underscores the growing tension between tech giants pushing AI adoption and institutions concerned about data privacy, control, and age-appropriateness for students. If you're an educator, parent, or just concerned about digital privacy, this is a critical development to watch. [Source: TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/04/duckduckgo-pitches-itself-to-schools-amid-backlash-over-googles-ai-policy/)

## AI in the Political Arena

AI isn't just changing how we work; it's increasingly shaping our political landscape. AI industry PACs spent millions trying to influence the 2026 midterms, with mixed results. Unsurprisingly, these political groups are already looking toward the next election cycle. [Source: TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/04/ai-pacs-spent-millions-trying-to-shape-congress-here%E2%80%99s-how-they-fared/)

Even more directly, OpenAI's Chris Lehane is already trying to define the 2028 election, proposing a "new social contract" that could benefit Americans while also advancing the AI giant's own business interests. This isn't just about tech anymore; it's about policy, regulation, and the very fabric of society. [Source: TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/04/openai-is-already-trying-to-shape-the-2028-election/)

The key takeaway here is that the AI industry isn't passively waiting for regulations; it's actively trying to shape the rules of the game. Staying informed about their lobbying efforts and proposed policies is crucial, as these will directly impact future AI development and its integration into our lives.
