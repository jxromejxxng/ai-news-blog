## AI in Education Gets Personal (and a Bit Virtual)

Ever wished you had a personal coach for your big presentation or a board meeting pitch? Well, Harvard Business School is making that a reality in their HBS Foundry program. For a cool $699, participants are getting feedback on their practice pitches and mock board meetings from AI avatars of their instructors.

Think about that for a second. This isn't just about watching a video lecture. This is AI stepping in to provide personalized, immediate feedback in a low-stakes environment. For anyone in education, corporate training, or even just looking to sharpen their professional skills, this is a huge deal. It points to a future where AI can scale personalized coaching and mentorship, making high-quality, practical feedback more accessible than ever. It's not replacing human instructors, but augmenting them in a really powerful way.

## The Race to Replicate: A New AI Breakthrough in Research

Big news on the research front! A new British AI lab called Inherent, founded by some brilliant minds from DeepMind, has just released an AI agent named Faraday. And Faraday isn't just good; it's reportedly *outperformed* giants like Anthropic and OpenAI in its ability to replicate scientific papers.

Why does this matter? Replicating research is a cornerstone of scientific progress, ensuring findings are robust and reliable. An AI that can do this effectively and efficiently could dramatically accelerate the pace of scientific discovery. For R&D departments, pharmaceutical companies, academic institutions, and really anyone involved in innovation, an AI 'teammate' like Faraday could mean faster breakthroughs, more reliable results, and a significant boost to productivity. This isn't just about writing code; it's about pushing the boundaries of human knowledge itself, faster than ever before.

## AI Safety Takes Center Stage: OpenAI's U-Turn and Industry Concerns

Finally, let's talk about safety – a topic that's getting more airtime as AI capabilities grow. In a surprising move, OpenAI, one of the leading AI labs, is now calling for California to *strengthen* its AI safety bill (SB 53), a bill they previously opposed.

This shift is significant. It suggests that even the developers at the forefront of AI are increasingly recognizing the need for robust guardrails. But here's the kicker: a new study reveals that many leading AI labs still don't have publicly documented plans for how they'd contain a "rogue model" – an AI system demonstrating unexpected and potentially dangerous behavior.

What does this mean for you and your business? Firstly, expect more regulation. OpenAI's stance indicates a growing consensus that self-regulation alone might not be enough. Companies developing or heavily relying on advanced AI need to pay close attention to these evolving legislative landscapes. Secondly, it highlights a practical concern: as AI gets more powerful, the ability to predict and control its behavior becomes paramount. The lack of clear containment plans from frontier labs is a stark reminder that while AI offers immense potential, it also comes with significant, unaddressed risks that the industry, and society, will need to grapple with sooner rather than later.
