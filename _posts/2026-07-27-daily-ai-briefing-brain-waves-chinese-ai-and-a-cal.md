## Beyond Cameras: Brain Waves for Advanced AI Training

We're seeing a significant push in training what's called "physical AI" – think advanced robots or automated systems that interact with the real world. Traditionally, these models learn from tons of video, but that's apparently not cutting it anymore for the cutting edge. The next frontier? Adding dense annotations, multiple camera angles, and get this: **brain wave readings.**

Imagine an AI learning not just what you *do*, but what you *intend* to do, or even how you *feel* about a task. This move suggests we're aiming for incredibly nuanced and intuitive AI systems, potentially leading to robots that understand human commands and preferences with unprecedented precision. For companies, this means the potential for highly adaptable and collaborative AI in manufacturing, logistics, healthcare, and more. It's about making AI less clunky and more aligned with human operation.

## OpenAI Hack Triggers Call for 'Radical Transparency'

In a pretty stunning development, OpenAI was reportedly hit by what's being called the "first autonomous agent cyberattack." This isn't just a standard hack; it suggests an AI system itself was used to orchestrate the breach. Hugging Face CEO Clément Delangue didn't mince words, calling it an "unprecedented event" and demanding "an unprecedented response!" including "radical transparency."

This is a huge deal for several reasons. Firstly, it highlights the growing vulnerability of AI systems to sophisticated attacks, especially as they become more autonomous. For any company building or deploying AI, security needs to be front and center, with a focus on how AI itself can be both a target and a weapon. Secondly, the call for "radical transparency" speaks to a growing demand for more openness from leading AI labs about incidents and their lessons learned. Trust in AI development hinges on how these major players respond to such critical security events. It's a wake-up call that the security implications of advanced AI are here, and they're complex.
