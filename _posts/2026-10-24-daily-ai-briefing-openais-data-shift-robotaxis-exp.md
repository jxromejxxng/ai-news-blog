## OpenAI Pumps the Brakes on Live Internet Training

First up, some big news from OpenAI that touches on AI safety and how these powerful models actually learn. OpenAI has announced it will no longer train its most advanced "frontier" AI models on the live internet. They're citing "significant risks" for this move, just weeks after one of their own engineers publicly questioned the viability of such a solution.

What does this mean for you and your business? Well, on one hand, it's a clear signal that AI developers are taking safety and potential biases seriously by moving towards more controlled, curated datasets. This could lead to more reliable, less unpredictable AI outputs. On the flip side, it might mean that the absolute latest, minute-by-minute internet happenings won't immediately factor into how these models learn. It's a trade-off: perhaps a slight lag in "freshness" for increased stability and safety. For companies building on OpenAI's tech, it's important to understand these data source limitations and how they might affect your applications. ## Robotaxis Rolling into Central Asia by 2027

Next, let's talk about robotaxis, because they're continuing their global march. InDrive, a major ride-hailing competitor to Uber, is setting its sights on Central Asia. They've announced plans to begin testing Pony.ai's robotaxis in Kazakhstan's capital in the first half of 2027, partnering with Nvidia to make it happen.

Why should this catch your eye? It's another solid indicator that self-driving technology isn't just for Silicon Valley or a few select cities anymore. Companies are actively exploring and investing in new markets, bringing autonomous mobility closer to a wider global audience. For local businesses and commuters in regions like Central Asia, this could mean new transportation options and potentially more efficient logistics in just a few years. It's a tangible step towards a more automated future, moving from concept to concrete deployment plans in new territories. ## Meta's Big AI Bet: A New Class of Stock

Finally, let's look at how big tech is financing its AI ambitions. Meta, the parent company of Facebook and Instagram, is reportedly planning to create a newly created class of stock specifically to fund its extensive AI buildout.

This move speaks volumes about the sheer scale and strategic importance of AI for Meta. Building and maintaining cutting-edge AI models and services isn't cheap – it requires massive computational power, top-tier talent, and continuous investment. By creating a dedicated stock class, Meta is essentially signaling to investors, "We are *all in* on AI, and we're restructuring our finances to ensure we win this race." For other companies, it's a stark reminder of the capital expenditure involved in competing at the forefront of AI development and the lengths major players are willing to go to secure their future in an AI-first world.
