## The Race for AI Chips Heats Up

The foundational layer of all this AI magic? Chips. And the competition is fierce. Britain's Fractile just secured a whopping \$720 million funding round, putting them squarely among the top-funded players in the race for purpose-built AI chips. Why should you care? More competition and innovation in chip design means more efficient, powerful, and potentially more affordable AI processing down the line. This is crucial for anyone building, deploying, or even just using AI tools, as better hardware means better performance.

## Funding AI Infrastructure: A Glimpse Behind the Curtain

Speaking of foundational layers, CoreWeave, a major player in providing GPU cloud infrastructure for AI, is getting creative with its funding. They're increasingly asking customers for prepayments, borrowing against specific contracts, and even looking at shorter-term, potentially higher-priced deals to fund their growth. This shows the immense capital demand in the AI infrastructure space. For businesses looking to scale their AI operations, this highlights the high cost of entry and the need for strategic planning around compute resources. It also suggests that getting your hands on high-demand GPUs might require more flexibility and upfront commitment.

## Practical AI for Internal Business Insights

Here's a really neat practical application: Databricks CEO Ali Ghodsi is using a newly revised AI agent called "Genie" to get the unvarnished truth about what's happening within his 12,000-employee company. He admits he can't know everyone, and people might not always tell him what's going wrong. But Genie can. This is a fantastic example of how AI isn't just for external products but can be a powerful internal tool for leadership to gain insights, identify bottlenecks, and understand company health in a way that traditional reporting might miss. Imagine an AI that helps you see your business more clearly – that's the kind of practical impact we're talking about.

## Making AI Agents More Reliable, Like Self-Driving Cars

And finally, on the development front, a company called Plurai is working on making AI agents more robust by borrowing a page from self-driving car development. They're using a similar approach to how end-to-end models were trained for autonomous vehicles, recognizing that AI agents are also probabilistic systems operating in unpredictable environments. This is crucial because for AI agents to truly be useful in our daily lives and work – whether scheduling meetings or managing complex projects – they need to be reliable and handle unexpected situations gracefully. This is about moving AI agents from cool demos to dependable tools.
[AI News & Artificial Intelligence | TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/11/plurai-wants-to-help-AI-agents-work-better-using-the-same-approach-that-helped-get-self-driving-cars-to-roads/)
