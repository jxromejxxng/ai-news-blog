## AI in the Physical World: Smarter Robots & Engineers

We're seeing some serious cash pouring into making AI work in the physical world, not just in software. This is where things get really interesting for industries that build, make, and design.

### Theker's Flexible Factory Bots

First up, Theker just snagged a cool $85 million to develop a new kind of factory robot. What makes these different? Most robots in factories are super specialized – they do one thing really well, like welding a specific car part. Theker's vision is for "generalist" robots that can be reconfigured for different tasks. Think of it like a multi-tool versus a single-purpose wrench. For manufacturers, this is a game-changer. It means more flexibility on production lines, less need for completely new equipment when product designs change, and potentially massive savings in setup time and cost. It's about making factories much more agile.

### Bezos-Backed 'Artificial General Engineer'

Speaking of big money, Jeff Bezos's venture, Prometheus, just raised a whopping $12 billion! This isn't for just any AI; they're aiming to build an "artificial general engineer" for the physical world. This company, now valued at $41 billion, wants to automate heavy engineering and even drug design. Imagine AI that can not only design complex structures or new medicines but also figure out how to build them in the real world. This has huge implications for industries like construction, infrastructure, and pharmaceuticals, potentially accelerating innovation and bringing down costs in areas that have historically been very slow and expensive.

## Navigating the Digital Landscape

AI isn't just building things; it's also helping us understand and interact with the digital content flooding our lives.

### Deezer's AI Music Detector

With the rise of AI-generated music, questions around authenticity and copyright are becoming more pressing. Deezer, the music streaming service, is stepping up with a new tool that can identify AI-generated music across platforms like Spotify and Apple Music. For artists, this is a big deal for protecting their work and ensuring fair play. For listeners, it means transparency – knowing whether the track you're grooving to was made by a human or an algorithm. And for the platforms themselves, it's a crucial step in content moderation and maintaining the integrity of their libraries.
[AI News & Artificial Intelligence | TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/11/deezers-new-tool-can-identify-ai-music-from-spotify-apple-music-and-others/)

### DoorDash Gets Chatty with AI

Finally, for a more everyday application, DoorDash is introducing "Ask DoorDash," a new AI chatbot. Instead of scrolling endlessly through restaurant menus, you'll be able to use natural language prompts and even photos to find exactly what you're looking for. Want "something spicy and vegetarian for dinner" or "a picture of that amazing pasta I had last week"? Just ask. This is a great example of how AI can simplify routine tasks, making apps more intuitive and user-friendly. For DoorDash, it's about improving the customer experience and potentially boosting engagement.
