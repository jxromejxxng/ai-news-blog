## Apple's AI Moment: Siri Gets Smart, Finally?

After what felt like ages, Apple finally showed its hand at WWDC 2026, putting a heavy dose of AI into its long-standing assistant, Siri, and launching "Apple Intelligence." The big takeaway? Apple's playing the long game, focusing on deeply integrated, practical AI that feels like it’s actually *part* of your device, not just an add-on. We're talking about a Siri that understands context better, can handle more complex requests, and generally feels more useful. This isn't about a flashy chatbot, but about making your iPhone and other devices genuinely smarter and more helpful in your daily tasks. It seems their "slow and steady" approach, focusing on a polished user experience, might be paying off.

## The AI Price Wars Heat Up: Cheaper Access for Everyone?

Good news for your wallet (and company budgets)! Google just significantly cut the price of its budget AI subscription tier. This move is a clear "warning shot" in the ongoing AI price wars. What this tells us is that the cost of accessing powerful AI is coming down, and fast. More broadly, tech companies are also looking for ways to handle AI workloads with cheaper models without sacrificing quality. If this trend continues, it means more affordable, high-quality AI tools for businesses and individuals alike, making advanced AI capabilities accessible to a much wider audience. For companies, this translates to potentially massive shifts in the economics of running AI-powered services.

## New AI on the Block: Anthropic's Claude Fable 5 Goes Public

Anthropic, a major player in the AI research space, has just released Claude Fable 5, making its "Mythos-class" model available to the public for the first time. This means another powerful, advanced AI model is now out there for us to experiment with and integrate into our workflows. Importantly, Anthropic is emphasizing safety, building in "guardrails" to block responses in high-risk areas like cybersecurity and biology. This highlights the industry's ongoing effort to balance powerful capabilities with responsible AI development.

## AI Gets Specific: Boosting Legal Teams

We're seeing AI get increasingly specialized, and a great example is Sandstone, which just raised $30 million to bring AI solutions to in-house legal teams. This isn't about replacing lawyers, but about empowering them. Imagine AI sifting through mountains of documents, identifying key information, or automating routine legal tasks. This kind of targeted application of AI can dramatically boost efficiency and accuracy in specialized fields, freeing up human experts to focus on more complex, strategic work.
[TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/09/sandstone-raises-30m-to-bring-ai-to-in-house-legal-teams/)

## Industry Watch: OpenAI's IPO & Sam Altman's Ventures

In big industry news, OpenAI is reportedly filing for an IPO. This is a massive step for one of the leading AI companies and will no doubt reshape the AI investment landscape. Meanwhile, Sam Altman's other venture, Tools for Humanity (the eye-scanning identity verification company), is reportedly facing revenue struggles and layoffs. This serves as a reminder that even with AI's rapid growth, not every related venture is an instant success, and the path to sustainable business models is still being forged.
