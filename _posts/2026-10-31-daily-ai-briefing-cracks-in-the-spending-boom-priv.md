Hey everyone, welcome to your Daily AI Briefing! It's Friday, October 31st, 2026, and we've got some interesting updates from the world of AI today. We're seeing some big shifts in how companies are spending on AI, what it means for privacy, and even a peek at how AI can simplify your next dinner outing. Let's dive in!

## The AI Spending Spree Hits a Speed Bump

For a while now, it's felt like companies were just throwing money at AI, building out massive infrastructure and making huge investments. But it looks like that honeymoon phase might be cooling down a bit. Reports are suggesting that while Big Tech is still pouring billions into AI, investors are getting a little more cautious. We're seeing concerns about mounting debt and negative cash flow, which is making people question how sustainable this rapid growth really is.
[TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/30/cracks-are-emerging-in-the-ai-spending-boom/)

Interestingly, even a titan like Nvidia, the company making the chips everyone needs for AI, is getting involved in a different way. They're actually considering cosigning loans for AI data centers, like the one Nscale is building in West Virginia. This move signals a couple of things: first, that even with high demand, securing funding for AI infrastructure can be tough, and second, that Nvidia is deeply invested in ensuring these projects get off the ground. It shows just how much is riding on these AI bets.
[TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/30/nvidia-weighs-playing-the-cosigner-for-nscales-ai-data-center-in-west-virginia/)

## A Reality Check for Privacy-First AI

In a market flooded with AI assistants, it’s tough to stand out. And sadly, it seems being 'privacy-focused' isn't always enough to keep the lights on. Ollie, an AI assistant built specifically to respect user privacy, is shutting down next week. This is a significant development because it highlights the challenges smaller players face when competing against tech giants with vast resources. It also raises questions about whether users are truly willing to pay or prioritize privacy enough for these dedicated services to thrive in the current landscape. It's a tough lesson for anyone hoping to build an AI product with privacy as its core differentiator.
[TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/30/ollie-a-privacy-focused-ai-assistant-is-shutting_down/)

## Your New AI Concierge for Restaurant Decisions

Okay, now for something a bit more directly useful for your daily life! Ever struggled to pick a restaurant with friends or family? Zest has a new AI agent that aims to solve this common dilemma. This AI concierge can take everyone's preferences into account, suggest options, put them to a vote, and then even book the reservation for you, all over text. This is a great example of how AI is moving beyond just complex coding or data analysis and into practical, everyday problem-solving that can genuinely save you time and hassle. Imagine never having another 'where do you want to eat?' debate again!
[TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/30/zests-new-ai-agent-finds-restaurants-and-books-reservations-over-text/)

## The Theranos Shadow Over the AI Boom

Finally, a crucial word of caution. Tyler Shultz, the whistleblower who helped expose the fraud at Theranos, is speaking out about the current AI boom. He warns that the excitement around AI presents 'a great opportunity to go deceive people.' His concern stems from the fact that many AI companies are raising capital at incredibly high valuations without having turned a profit. It’s a stark reminder that while AI offers immense potential, we need to be critically evaluating the claims and financial health of companies in this space. Transparency and healthy skepticism are more important than ever to avoid repeating past mistakes.
[TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/30/theranos-whistleblower-tyler-shultz-warns-that-the-ai-boom-is-a-great-opportunity-to-go-deceive-people/)
