### OpenAI's AI Dives Deep into Advanced Math

This is a big one for anyone interested in the raw power of AI. OpenAI has just formed a new math advisory group, and here's the kicker: their AI has already resolved over 100 open mathematical problems. While the advisory group is there to guide, it's clear they're not looking to slow down this incredible pace.

**Why this matters for you:** This isn't just academic navel-gazing. AI's ability to solve complex, previously intractable math problems hints at its immense potential in scientific research, engineering, financial modeling, and any field that relies heavily on advanced analytical problem-solving. If you're in R&D or a data-intensive industry, imagine AI helping you crack problems faster than ever before. It's about accelerating discovery and innovation.

### Meta's AI Agent "Muse" Makes a Splash (and Hits a Wall)

Meta's new AI agent, Muse, is making waves on mobile. According to Appfigures, Muse has racked up more downloads and daily active users in the U.S. and Canada than ChatGPT did in its initial mobile launch period. That's some serious momentum, showing Meta's push into the AI assistant space is resonating with users.
[AI News & Artificial Intelligence | TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/21/metas-muse-is-outpacing-chatgpts-early-mobile-launch/)

However, not all doors are open for Muse. Amazon has reportedly blocked Meta's AI agent from accessing Amazon.com. Given Amazon has its own AI models and platforms, it's not surprising they're keeping their digital storefront exclusive.

**What this means for you and your business:** For users, more options for AI agents on your phone means more ways to get tasks done, find information, and interact with AI. For businesses, this dual news highlights a crucial point: the AI agent market is fiercely competitive, and the "walled garden" approach is real. If you're developing an AI product or integrating AI into your services, you need to consider where your AI can and cannot operate, and the implications of platform competition on reach and functionality.

### Is AI Coming for Your Accountant's Job?

Here's a story that hits close to home for many professionals. A former accountant has launched Tabby, an AI-powered tool designed to make traditional accounting roles "obsolete." Tabby acts as a real-time bookkeeping interface, handling paperwork and providing up-to-the-minute profit and loss data for businesses.
[AI News & Artificial Intelligence | TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/21/with-tabby-a-former-accountant-is-using-ai-to-make-accountants-obsolete/)

**Your takeaway:** This isn't just about accountants; it's a preview of how AI will impact many professional services. For businesses, tools like Tabby promise significant efficiency gains, real-time financial insights, and potentially lower operational costs. For those in accounting (or similar fields), it's a clear signal to adapt. This isn't necessarily about job *elimination* for everyone, but a strong push towards *redefinition*. The human element will likely shift from rote data entry to higher-level advisory, strategic analysis, and client relationship management.
