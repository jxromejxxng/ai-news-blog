## Signal Boosts Privacy Against AI Surveillance

First up, a big move from Signal, the popular encrypted messaging app. They're now limiting users' ability to take screenshots within their Android and iOS apps. Why? This is a direct response to what they call an "existential threat" of AI surveillance.

**Why this matters to you:** In an age where AI can analyze images and text at lightning speed, restricting screenshots adds another layer of privacy to your conversations. For individuals, it means more secure chats, knowing sensitive information is less likely to be captured and processed by external AI tools. For companies and organizations that rely on secure communication, this highlights the growing need for robust privacy features in the face of advanced AI capabilities.

[TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/21/signal-restricts-mobile-screenshots-as-it-confronts-existential-threat-of-ai-surveillance/)

## Your Next AI Assistant Might Live in Your Text Messages

Ever wish you had a smart assistant that just lived in your texts? Well, that's becoming a reality. We're seeing a rise in AI agents designed to integrate directly into your messaging apps. These aren't just general chatbots; they range from personal assistants to specialized agents for families, travel planning, or work tasks.

**How you can use this:** Imagine quickly asking an AI agent in your group chat to find a restaurant, book a flight, or even help coordinate a family event, all without leaving your conversation. For individuals, it's about unparalleled convenience and instant access to AI assistance. For businesses, this opens up new avenues for customer service, internal team collaboration, or even delivering personalized recommendations directly where your customers are.

[TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/21/all-the-ai-agents-that-can-live-in-your-text-messages/)

## Selling Your Face to AI: The Future of Hollywood?

Here's one that sounds straight out of a sci-fi movie: actors are now licensing their faces to AI startups. This trend, already popular in China with "microdramas," is starting to make waves in Hollywood. Essentially, an actor sells the rights to their digital likeness, allowing an AI to generate their face for various productions.

**What this means for actors and creators:** On one hand, it could offer actors a new revenue stream, allowing their digital doubles to appear in countless projects without requiring their physical presence. For content creators and studios, it could revolutionize production, offering efficiency and scalability for digital characters. However, experts are rightly questioning the long-term consequences for intellectual property, fair compensation, and the very nature of acting. It's a fascinating, complex development that will undoubtedly reshape the entertainment industry.
