### Push for Public Control Over AI

First up, we're seeing growing calls for more public involvement in how AI is developed and regulated. New York's Mayor Zohran Mamdani is urging New Yorkers to push back against the idea that AI's impact is inevitable and beyond our control. He's advocating for regulations that protect the public and ensure power remains in our hands, not just with big tech. This isn't just a local issue; it's a conversation happening globally. For businesses, this means staying aware of potential shifts in policy and public sentiment, as future regulations could significantly impact how AI is deployed and used. For individuals, it's a call to engage and voice your concerns about AI's role in society.

### AI Security: A Growing Concern for Everyone

Security around AI is becoming a major headline. Microsoft is sounding the alarm, comparing AI agents used by employees to "insider threats." Their message? You can't blindly trust all "frontier models," especially those not controlled by major AI labs. This is a huge deal for businesses, as it highlights the need for robust security protocols and careful vetting of AI tools employees integrate into their workflows. Think about it: if an AI agent has access to sensitive company data, it needs to be as secure as any human employee.

Speaking of security, the open-source AI coding tool OpenCode recently got hit by hackers. They stole users' API keys, leading to unauthorized charges on AI accounts. What's even more concerning is that the AI tools themselves reportedly refused to analyze the malicious code, leaving users vulnerable. This is a stark reminder for developers and companies using open-source AI tools: the convenience comes with a responsibility to understand and mitigate potential security risks. Always keep an eye on your API keys and consider dedicated security measures for your AI integrations.

### The Rise of Personal AI Companions

On a lighter note, personal AI companions are continuing their rapid ascent. Tolan, an AI companion app, just secured another $30 million in funding after attracting over 250,000 active users. These companions are designed to help users navigate daily life, offering personalized assistance.

And it's not just Tolan. Eugenia Kuyda, the founder behind the popular Replika AI, is back with a new venture called Wabi. Wabi offers a free, downloadable personal AI assistant, building on an idea she had over a decade ago. These developments show a clear trend: AI is becoming more personalized and accessible, moving beyond enterprise tools into our everyday lives to offer support and companionship. For individuals, these tools offer new ways to organize, learn, and even connect.

### Using AI to Take Back Control of Your Tech

Finally, here's a fascinating twist: people are now using AI to "dumb down" their smart TVs! Fed up with intrusive ads, constant tracking, and sluggish interfaces, some smart TV owners are deploying AI agents to strip away bloatware and regain control over their viewing experience. This is a brilliant example of users leveraging AI not just for new capabilities, but to solve existing pain points and reclaim privacy and simplicity. It highlights a powerful trend: AI can be a tool for empowerment and user agency, helping us tailor our tech to *our* needs, rather than letting our tech dictate to us.
