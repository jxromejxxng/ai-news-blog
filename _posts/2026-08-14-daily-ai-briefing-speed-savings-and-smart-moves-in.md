## OpenAI's Need for Speed: Ultrafast Mode Arrives

Big news for anyone using OpenAI's models in a demanding environment: they're rolling out "Ultrafast," a new mode for their latest and most powerful model, GPT-5.6 Sol. What does that mean for you? It means a staggering **14x speed increase**! If you're a business relying on AI for quick responses, real-time analysis, or high-volume tasks, this is a game-changer. Imagine customer service bots responding almost instantly or complex data processing finishing in a fraction of the time. OpenAI is clearly pushing for enterprise adoption, and speed is a huge selling point.
[TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/13/openai-introduces-ultrafast-a-new-mode-that-makes-gpt-5-6-sol-work-at-14x-the-speed/)

## Making AI More Affordable: Writer's New Model Cuts Token Costs

While speed is great, cost is always a factor, especially for businesses scaling their AI usage. That's where Writer comes in. They've just introduced a new AI model, built on a variation of Z.ai's open-source GLM-5.2. The big takeaway here is that this new system promises **deployment-ready capabilities at a much lower price**. This is fantastic news for companies (and even individual developers) who want powerful AI without breaking the bank. Lower token costs mean more experimentation, more applications, and broader access to advanced AI tools.
[TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/13/writer-introduces-new-ai-model-and-upgraded-harness-to-contain-token-costs/)

## IBM Jumps Deeper into Enterprise AI with OpenAI Partnership

Speaking of enterprise, IBM is making a significant push to bolster its AI offerings by partnering directly with OpenAI. As part of this deal, IBM plans to **train and certify tens of thousands of consultants on OpenAI's technologies**. This isn't just a handshake agreement; it's a strategic move to integrate OpenAI's capabilities directly into IBM's vast client base. For businesses looking to adopt AI but needing expert guidance, this partnership means a more robust, skilled workforce ready to help deploy and manage these advanced systems. It's a huge vote of confidence in OpenAI's tools for serious business applications.
[TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/13/ibm-partners-with-openai-to-bolster-enterprise-ai-push/)

## The Price of Progress: Databricks' Massive Funding Round

Ever wonder why so much money is pouring into AI? Databricks gives us a clear answer. They just closed a massive funding round, settling on **$5 billion at an eye-watering $190 billion valuation**. While they initially sought $1 billion, investors were so keen they wanted to put in $15 billion! Databricks CEO Ali Ghodsi put it simply to TechCrunch: "AI is expensive." This enormous investment highlights the immense capital required to build and scale cutting-edge AI, but also the equally immense belief in its future returns. It shows that the industry is still in a major growth phase, with investors betting big on the companies that can deliver powerful AI solutions.
[TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/13/databricks-wanted-to-raise-1b-investors-wanted-15b-it-settled-on-5b-at-a-190b-valuation/)

## Microsoft's AI Spring Cleaning: Streamlining Copilot

On the user experience front, Microsoft is making some practical adjustments to its Copilot offerings. They're **merging their separate consumer and business Copilot apps** into a single, unified experience. This is great news for users who might have been confused by multiple versions. They're also **killing off some less successful AI-generated features** like podcasts, Group Chats, Deep Research, and the Mico character. This move tells us that companies are learning what works and what doesn't in the real world. It's about refining, simplifying, and focusing on the AI features that truly deliver value and a smooth user experience.
[TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/13/microsoft-kills-off-unsuccessful-ai-features-while-merging-its-separate-copilot-apps/)
