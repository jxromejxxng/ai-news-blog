## The Hunt for 'Real' AI Innovation – Beyond the Wrappers

If you're an aspiring AI founder or just following the startup scene, this first piece of news is a big one. Google and Accel, two major players in the tech investment world, recently reviewed over 4,000 AI startup pitches for their Atoms accelerator cohort in India. What did they find? A whopping 70% of them were what they call "AI wrappers."

**What's an "AI wrapper"?** Think of it like this: you take a powerful existing AI model (like ChatGPT or another large language model), put a slightly different user interface on it, and maybe add a few minor features. It's essentially packaging someone else's core AI technology without adding significant, proprietary innovation or solving a deep, unique problem.

Google and Accel are clearly looking for more. They're cutting through the noise to find startups that are building foundational tech, leveraging unique datasets, or solving complex problems in novel ways. This trend shows that investors are becoming savvier; the days of getting funding just for putting a new coat of paint on an existing LLM might be fading. For founders, the message is clear: dig deeper, innovate harder, and focus on proprietary value.

## Urgent Warnings on AI Safety and Mental Health

Now for a more serious and critical development. A lawyer who has handled cases linking AI chatbots to psychosis is sounding the alarm, warning that these technologies could pose "mass casualty risks." We've known for years about concerns linking AI chatbots to suicides, but the potential for them to be involved in mass casualty events is a terrifying escalation.

The core issue, according to this lawyer, is that the technology is advancing at an incredible pace, far outstripping the safeguards, regulations, and ethical frameworks designed to keep us safe. While AI offers immense benefits, we're clearly facing a very real, very urgent need to address its potential downsides, especially concerning mental health and societal safety.

For anyone interacting with AI chatbots, this is a stark reminder to approach them with a critical mind. Don't take everything they say as gospel, especially when dealing with sensitive personal issues. And for developers and policymakers, it's a call to action: we need robust safety protocols, clearer ethical guidelines, and perhaps even some regulatory slowing down to ensure human well-being remains paramount.
